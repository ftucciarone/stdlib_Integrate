\section*{Example: the Arenstorf orbit}

\subsection*{1. Problem}
From the SciML problem library (\texttt{prob\_ode\_threebody}), after Hairer,
N{\o}rsett and Wanner (\emph{Solving ODEs I}, section II.0): a light body
(a spacecraft) moves in the plane of the Earth and the Moon, described in the
frame that rotates with them, the Earth at $(-\mu, 0)$, the Moon at
$(1 - \mu, 0)$, $\mu' = 1 - \mu$:
\begin{align*}
  \ddot y_1 &= y_1 + 2\dot y_2 - \mu'\frac{y_1 + \mu}{D_1} - \mu\frac{y_1 - \mu'}{D_2}, &
  D_1 &= \bigl((y_1 + \mu)^2 + y_2^2\bigr)^{3/2},\\
  \ddot y_2 &= y_2 - 2\dot y_1 - \mu'\frac{y_2}{D_1} - \mu\frac{y_2}{D_2}, &
  D_2 &= \bigl((y_1 - \mu')^2 + y_2^2\bigr)^{3/2}.
\end{align*}
The terms $\pm 2\dot y$ are the Coriolis force and $y_1, y_2$ the centrifugal
force of the rotating frame. The initial values lie on a periodic orbit
(Arenstorf, 1963) of period $T$; there is no closed-form solution, but after
$T$ the body is back at its start, so the closing error
$|u(T) - u(0)|$ is the global error. The orbit passes close to the Moon and
the Earth, where the motion is fast, and is very sensitive to errors there.

The reference (dashed) starts at $(0.994, 0)$, just beyond the Moon, winds
around the Earth in several lobes and comes back to the Moon, where it
closes. (a) With 4000 equal steps no method follows

onto a different path. (b) With 64000 steps the higher-order methods follow
the orbit closely, while \texttt{rk3ws} still leaves it.

\subsection*{3. Tests: closing error and cost}

\paragraph{Prediction.} Every method converges at its general order once the
step resolves the passages near the Moon; before that, the error is of order
one and no order can be seen. Since equal steps must be small everywhere to
be small enough near the Moon, the cost of a given accuracy falls steeply
with the order.

(i) Every method reaches its general order, but only at small
steps: the closing error drops below one between $16\,000$ and $32\,000$ steps
for \texttt{rk4}, between $8000$ and $16\,000$ for \texttt{dp54}, and only
after hundreds of thousands for \texttt{rk3ws} (order 2 here). The cut-off for the observed
order is $10^{-8}$: on this sensitive orbit round-off accumulates to about
$10^{-9}$ over millions of steps (the reference itself closes to that
level). (ii) Along the orbit the error grows in jumps, at the close passages where
the motion is fastest, the first and last of them near the Moon.
For a closing error below $10^{-3}$, \texttt{dp54} needs $192\,000$
evaluations of $f$, \texttt{rk4} $512\,000$, while \texttt{rk3ws} does not get there
within four million steps.
Equal steps are what makes this expensive: Hairer et al.\ make the same point
with this orbit, which a method with step-size control follows with far fewer
steps, small ones only near the Moon.
