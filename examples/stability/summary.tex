\section*{Example: stability limit on the real axis}

\subsection*{1. Problem}
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{dy}{dt} = -y, \qquad y(0) = 1,
  \qquad\text{exact solution}\qquad y(t) = e^{-t} \to 0 .
\end{equation*}
The exact solution decays for every $t$; the question is how large the step
$h = \Delta t$ can be before the numerical solution stops decaying. One step
multiplies $y$ by the stability polynomial of the method,
\begin{equation*}
  y_{n+1} = R(-h)\, y_n, \qquad
  R(z) = 1 + z\, b^{\mathsf T} (I - zA)^{-1} \mathbf{1},
\end{equation*}
computed here from the Butcher tableau alone, independently of the step
routines.

($R(-h^*) = -1$, $|y|$ drawn): the limit is sharp.

\subsection*{3. Test: the stability limit $h^*$}

\paragraph{Prediction.} The run stays bounded exactly while
$|R(-h)| \le 1$. The limit $h^*$ is the first $h$ where $|R(-h)|$ exceeds 1:
2 for every first- and second-order method, $\approx 2.51$ for third order,
$\approx 2.79$ for RK4.
